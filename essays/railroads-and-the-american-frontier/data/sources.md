## The Government's Bet

### Pacific Railroad Acts — Wikipedia
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Railroad_Acts
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - 1862 Act: 6,400 acres land grant per mile of track (5 alternate sections per mile, each side); $16K/mile bonds on level ground, $32K in foothills, $48K in mountains
  - 1864 Act: doubled the land grant to 12,800 acres/mile; doubled bond rates ($32K/$48K/$64K)
  - "Checkerboard" land pattern: alternate sections, with government keeping intervening sections
- **Relevance:** Primary structure of the government bet. Direct source for Section 2.

### Pacific Railway Act (1862) — National Archives
- **URL:** https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/pacific-railway-act
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Signed by Lincoln July 1, 1862
  - Authorized Union Pacific (eastward from Sacramento via Central Pacific) and westward from Missouri River
  - Government bonds were 30-year, 6% — effectively a federal loan, not a grant
- **Relevance:** Primary source for the legislative framework.

### Railroad Land Grants in the United States — Wikipedia
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_land_grants_in_the_United_States
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - **~131 million acres** of federal land granted (≈7% of continental US), to ~80 railroad companies, 1850-1871
  - Adding state grants brings total to ~180 million acres
  - Federal land grant program effectively ended 1871
  - **Discrepancy with outline:** outline says 175M; correct federal-only figure is 131M, federal+state ~180M
- **Relevance:** Major correction needed in draft. Use 131M federal / 180M total.

## Railroad Finance

### Railroads and the Transformation of Capitalism — Harvard Business School
- **URL:** https://www.library.hbs.edu/hc/railroads/finance.html
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Railroad capitalization (stocks + bonds): $4.6B (1876) → $10.6B (1890)
  - For comparison, US national debt was ~$1.2B in 1890
  - Railroads were >50% of NYSE capitalization and >80% of trading volume from Civil War to ~1900
  - Transportation companies were the largest US corporations until Standard Oil overtook in 1884
- **Relevance:** Source for the "60% of NYSE" claim — actually >50% of cap, >80% of trades. Anchors Section 3 on the money.

### 200 Years of US Stock Market Sectors — Visual Capitalist
- **URL:** https://www.visualcapitalist.com/200-years-u-s-stock-market-sectors/
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Railroads dominated US equity markets from 1840s through ~1900
  - First US stock to be widely traded: Vermont Central Railroad, 1845
- **Relevance:** Visual reference for sector composition over time.

### Crédit Mobilier Scandal — Wikipedia / Library of Congress
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9dit_Mobilier_scandal
- **URL:** https://guides.loc.gov/this-month-in-business-history/september/Credit-Mobilier-Scandal
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Construction company set up by Union Pacific insiders to bill UP at inflated prices
  - Actual construction cost: ~$50M; billed: ~$94M; skimmed: ~$44M
  - Oakes Ames (Congressman, MA) sold discounted stock to colleagues to buy political protection
  - Exposed September 1872 by NY Sun during Grant re-election campaign
  - Implicated: future President Garfield, Speaker Blaine, VP Schuyler Colfax
  - House censured Ames + Brooks Feb 27, 1873
- **Relevance:** Centerpiece corruption story for Section 3.

### Panic of 1873 — Wikipedia / Smithsonian
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_of_1873
- **URL:** https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/robber-baron-gamble-railroads-brought-down-bank-plunged-us-into-first-great-depression-panic-1873-180982877/
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Trigger: Jay Cooke & Co. failed Sept 18, 1873, unable to market Northern Pacific bonds
  - Compounded by Crédit Mobilier-induced distrust of railroad securities
  - European capital markets simultaneously contracting → Cooke could not place bonds abroad
  - Triggered the "Long Depression" — some scholars extend it through the 1890s
- **Relevance:** Boom-bust pattern. Direct railroad cause for the worst US depression of the 19th century.

### Railroad Capital Investment Detail — NBER / Fabricated Knowledge
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c3025/c3025.pdf
- **URL:** https://www.fabricatedknowledge.com/p/lessons-from-history-the-great-railroad
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - ~$8B capital invested in railroads by 1890
  - Estimated ~40% of 1890 railroad capitalization was "watered" stock — securities issued in excess of underlying physical investment
  - Total stocks + bonds: $10.6B by 1890/1897
- **Relevance:** Quantifies the financial bubble inside the infrastructure boom.

## Labor

### Chinese Railroad Workers Project — Stanford
- **URL:** https://web.stanford.edu/group/chineserailroad/cgi-bin/website/virtual/
- **URL:** https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2019/04/giving-voice-to-chinese-railroad-workers
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - 10,000-15,000 Chinese workers at construction peaks; up to ~20,000 cumulative 1865-1869
  - Constituted ~90% of Central Pacific workforce for much of construction
  - Death rate exceeded 10% during Sierra Nevada construction phase
  - At least 1,000 Chinese laborers died during transcontinental construction (avalanches, explosions, exposure)
  - Key brutal section: 15 tunnels through Sierra Nevada; Summit Tunnel was the worst
  - Paid less than white workers; struck for equal pay June 1867 (failed)
- **Relevance:** Section 4 anchor. Confirms the outline's 10K-15K and ~90% figures.

### 1867 Chinese Labor Strike — Wikipedia
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1867_Chinese_Labor_Strike
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - June 25, 1867: ~3,000 Chinese workers struck in Sierra Nevada
  - Demanded $40/month (vs $30-35) and 10-hour day
  - CP cut off food/supplies; strike collapsed within a week
- **Relevance:** Concrete labor-history detail for Section 4.

## The Contrarian View (Fogel)

### Robert Fogel — Wikipedia / EH.net
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fogel
- **URL:** https://eh.net/book_reviews/railroads-and-american-economic-growth-essays-in-econometric-history/
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Book: *Railroads and American Economic Growth: Essays in Econometric History* (1964)
  - Headline finding: per-capita income reached on Jan 1, 1890 would have been reached by Mar 31, 1890 *without* railroads (≈3-month delay)
  - Agricultural sector "social savings" estimate: 2.7% of 1890 GNP (0.6% interregional + 2.1% intraregional)
  - Other estimates including all sectors: 6-7% of GNP
  - Counterfactual: hypothetical canal/wagon system that would have been built absent railroads
  - Won 1993 Nobel (with Douglass North)
  - **Outline question "was it 5% of GDP?"** — answer: 2.7% for agriculture only; 6-7% if broader. The often-cited "5%" is a rough midpoint.
- **Relevance:** The contrarian Section 6 hinges on this. Fogel's number is smaller than the outline guessed.

### NBER Working Paper — Donaldson & Hornbeck on the Fogel Critique
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19213/w19213.pdf
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Modern reanalysis using county-level land values
  - Estimates US GDP would have been 3.22% lower in 1890 without railroads
  - Substantially higher than Fogel's 2.7% but still in the same order of magnitude
  - Suggests Fogel underestimated by missing land-value channel
- **Relevance:** Lets Section 6 be more nuanced — modern econometric pushback on Fogel without abandoning the core "smaller than expected" finding.

### Railroad Redux — Minneapolis Fed
- **URL:** https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2001/railroad-redux
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Plain-language summary of the social-savings debate
  - Reviews critiques of Fogel's counterfactual canal assumptions
- **Relevance:** Useful framing source — readable take on a technical debate.

## Second-Order Effects

### Standard Time / Day of Two Noons
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_time_in_the_United_States
- **URL:** https://historymatters.gmu.edu/d/5748
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Pre-1883: ~144 local time zones in North America
  - General Time Convention, Oct 11, 1883 (Grand Pacific Hotel, Chicago): adopted William F. Allen's 5-zone proposal based on 75th/90th/105th/120th meridians
  - "Day of Two Noons": Nov 18, 1883 — clocks reset at noon to new standard
  - Federal law: Standard Time Act, March 19, 1918 (35 years after railroads imposed it)
- **Relevance:** Strongest example of "infrastructure forcing institutional change." Section 5 lead.

### Union Stock Yards & Chicago Meatpacking
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Stock_Yards
- **URL:** https://www.wttw.com/chicago-stories/union-stockyards/the-union-stockyards-a-story-of-american-capitalism
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Founded by a consortium of 9 railroads, opened Christmas Day 1865
  - Throughput: 2M animals/year (1870) → 9M (1890)
  - 1865-1900: ~400M livestock processed
  - By 1900: 25,000 employees, 82% of US domestic meat
  - Peaked 1924, employed 40,000 by 1921
- **Relevance:** Concrete numbers showing how railroads concentrated an industry geographically.

### Swift Refrigerator Line / Refrigerated Cars
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swift_Refrigerator_Line
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavus_Franklin_Swift
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Gustavus Swift hired engineer Andrew Chase, 1878, to design practical refrigerated car
  - First units delivered 1880; ~200 cars within a year
  - "Era of cheap beef" — dressed-beef shipping replaced live cattle transport
  - Decoupled slaughter from market location → Chicago monopoly
- **Relevance:** Shows the second-order tech (refrigerated cars) reshaping diet and geography.

### Sears, Roebuck & Montgomery Ward
- **URL:** https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/sears-postal-service-catalogs
- **URL:** https://www.wttw.com/chicago-stories/rise-and-fall-of-the-mail-order-giants/the-rise-and-fall-of-chicagos-mail-order-giants
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Montgomery Ward founded 1872 — first general-merchandise mail-order catalog
  - Sears: founded 1886 (watches), full company 1893 (with Roebuck)
  - Both Chicago-based — railroad hub was the enabler
  - Rural Free Delivery rolled out nationally 1902 — completed the rail-to-doorstep loop
- **Relevance:** First nationwide retail. Direct downstream consequence of rail + postal infrastructure.

### Telegraph + Railroad Coupling
- **URL:** https://eh.net/encyclopedia/history-of-the-u-s-telegraph-industry/
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_transcontinental_telegraph
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - Telegraph lines built along railroad rights-of-way by mutual agreement
  - Railroads provided depot space + free transport for line repair crews
  - Western Union got monopoly route protection; railroads got free dispatch service
  - Transcontinental telegraph completed Oct 1861 — 8 years before transcontinental railroad
  - Western Union controlled 90% of US telegraph traffic by 1866
- **Relevance:** Communication infrastructure literally piggybacked on transport infrastructure. Section 5.

### Standard Oil & Railroad Rebates
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Improvement_Company
- **URL:** https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/rockefellers-south/
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - 1868 Lake Shore RR / Rockefeller deal: ~71% effective discount on listed rates in exchange for guaranteed 60 carloads/day
  - South Improvement Company (1871): secret cartel of railroads + refiners; Rockefeller member; charter revoked before any oil shipped, after public outrage ("Cleveland Massacre")
  - Subsequent rebate deals enabled Standard Oil to drive competitors out — by 1882, Standard refined ~90% of US oil
- **Relevance:** Single best example of railroad bargaining power creating downstream monopolies.

### Civil War Military Railroads
- **URL:** https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2011/summer/usmrr.html
- **URL:** https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/10-facts-railroads-civil-war
- **Accessed:** 2026-05-03
- **Key data:**
  - US Military Railroads (USMRR) under D.C. McCallum operated up to 2,105 miles by war's end
  - Sherman's Atlanta campaign (1864): supplied 100,000-strong army via Western & Atlantic from Louisville
  - Sherman's quote: railroad value "fully recognized in war quite as much as, if not more so than, in peace"
  - Union's logistical superiority a primary factor in victory
- **Relevance:** Proves railroad's strategic role before the western buildout. Section 5.
